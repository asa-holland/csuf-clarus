How Confidence Scores Work

All scores use a 0-5 scale derived mechanically from the number and strength of signals found during analysis. No subjective judgment is applied.

The raw signal value (0.0-1.0) is converted by: score = round(raw × 5), clamped to the range 0-5.

ScoreWhat it means
0 / 5No supporting signals detected; result is a best-guess fallback.
1 / 5Single weak signal; very low certainty.
2 / 5One moderate signal, or several weak ones.
3 / 5Multiple independent signals; reasonable confidence.
4 / 5Strong evidence from multiple independent sources.
5 / 5Maximum confidence; all expected signals present.

How signals are counted per context

Document Segments - counts how many normative or informative keyword patterns appear in the segment text (e.g. shall, must, should, example markers). More matching patterns raises the score.

Semantic Profiles - counts how many of the 6 possible linguistic anchors were successfully extracted: Subject, Object, Modality, Condition, Temporal, and Negation. The average extraction confidence of those anchors scales the result.

Contradictions - reflects which detection method triggered. Negated-verb matches (e.g. learn vs. do not learn) and outcome-antonym pairs score 4-5. Modal-keyword contradictions (e.g. must vs. must not) score 3-4.

Average scores - the arithmetic mean of all individual scores in a section, displayed to one decimal place.
